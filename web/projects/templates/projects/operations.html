Early setup

People counting drafts

Save a private draft now and return to it later. Recorded-video replay is not available yet; these drafts cannot count people or connect to a camera.

How this fits with the rest of Athena. A photo inspection answers “who is visible in these pictures?” for a handful of still frames. A custom detector teaches the computer to find people in any picture you give it — and an approved detector is one of the parts an operation draft can pin. An operation here goes further: a private, saved setup that will eventually count crossings from recorded video. Counting is not available yet; this page and the docs wiki say exactly what works today.

Set up an operation, one step at a time

Each step has one clear next action. Your draft saves as you go, and you can pick up where you left off after a reload. Nothing here counts people yet.

    Create or edit a draft

    Save the name first, then add one private recording and mark a line on its picture. Testing counts is still being built. Drafts are visible only to their creator.

    Test recording

    Add an H.264 MP4 up to 50 MB, 5 minutes and 1920×1080 pixels. The recording is stored privately. This checks its file information only; replay analysis and counting are not available yet.

    Draw and name the counting line

    Pause the recording at a clear view, then choose two points on the picture. Name the line and label the two sides of it — your own names for the sides, like "Street" and "Shop"; Athena does not decide which side means what. The arrow points towards the side you name in "Movement towards the arrow". You can also enter point positions below using percentages from the top-left corner.

    Move the endpoints with the keyboard

    First choose an endpoint with its buttons, then move it with the four arrow buttons: one press is a fine step of 0.001 of the picture, and holding Shift makes it a coarse step of 0.01. The endpoint never leaves the picture, so a step that would cross an edge is held at that edge. Arrow keys also work directly in the position fields. Dragging an endpoint is never required.

    Undo and redo

    Undo steps back through this session's endpoint moves, direction reversals and side-label changes, one change at a time, up to the point where the line was last saved or loaded. Redo puts a step back. Saving the draft does not erase the session's history, but undo never reaches past the last saved state into older work.

    Direction

    The direction runs from the start point to the end point. Reversing it is one explicit action: the two endpoints swap and your side labels move with them, so the names keep pointing at the same physical sides while the arrow flips.

    Check the direction with an example

    This example shows a person walking from the side you named against the arrow, across the line, to the side you named towards the arrow. It is an illustration of your own labelling — not a detection and not a promise about counts.

    Mark expected crossings

    Use this as a manual reference for later testing. Mark each real crossing while the recording plays. These are your labels, not Athena detections or an automatic count.

      Draw and name bays

      A bay is one named four-corner area on the same picture, like one parking space. Add a bay, set its four corners as percentages from the top-left corner, and name it — the name is yours to change later; a bay's identity is its stable ID, so renaming a bay never loses its history. An outline that crosses itself ("a bowtie") is refused — a bay's outline must not overlap itself — and corners always stay inside the picture.

      Move the corners with the keyboard

      First choose a corner with its buttons, then move it with the four arrow buttons: one press is a fine step of 0.001 of the picture, and holding Shift makes it a coarse step of 0.01. The corner never leaves the picture, so a step that would cross an edge is held at that edge. Arrow keys also work directly in the position fields. Dragging a corner is never required.
