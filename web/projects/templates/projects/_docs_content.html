00 Overview

How Athena OS works

Teach AI what matters to your world

Athena OS is an open-source, self-hostable AI training and inspection platform designed so teams without a machine learning background can build, train, evaluate, improve and deploy models on their own data. While image object detection is the primary verified local workflow today, the platform centers the training task rather than the camera or file format.

{% icon "sparkle" 18 %}1. Instant visual inspections

Ask a question in plain words, add up to 6 photos or a short video, and get a plain-language report with verdicts, counts, marked regions, and next steps. No training data needed.

Start an inspection{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

{% icon "detector" 18 %}2. No-code custom model training

Follow six consistent steps across supported modalities: choose a goal, add examples, teach with labels, train the model, test against reality, and use and improve safely.

Open custom projects{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

{% icon "activity" 18 %}3. People counting operations

For questions a photo cannot answer — how many people crossed a line — save a private counting setup from recorded video. Being built now; counting is not available yet.

Open people counting{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

Shared lifecycle across every modality: Project, Task, Observation, Annotation, Dataset Version, Training Run, Model Version, Evaluation, Deployment, and Feedback.

01 Getting started

First-run setup & sign-in

Admins & operators
  1. Complete first-run onboarding: On a brand-new install with no users yet, visiting the app opens the guided setup screen (/setup/). Enter the first administrator email, username, and password.
  2. Pick your starting AI option: During setup, choose Local Qwen (Ollama) (defaults to http://127.0.0.1:11434/v1), enter a Custom AI endpoint, or select Skip for now and configure it later in settings.
  3. Sign in anytime: Returning users sign in at /login/. Repeated failed sign-in attempts temporarily lock out the IP/username to protect your server.
  4. Switch workspace theme: Use Control-room mode at the bottom of the left sidebar to toggle between the bright day theme and the dark control-room theme.

02 AI configuration

Connect your AI model

Open AI model{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

Before running an instant inspection, Athena needs a vision-language model (a model that can read images). The status dot next to AI model in the sidebar turns green when it is answering.

  1. Open AI model settings: Click AI model in the sidebar (/settings/ai).
  2. Choose Built-in Qwen or Your own AI:
    • Built-in Qwen: Uses the organisation-wide server configured by your Athena administrator. Click Test the built-in model to verify it responds.
    • Your own AI: Connect any OpenAI-compatible /v1/chat/completions vision service (OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter, Ollama, vLLM, or LM Studio). Pick a preset or enter the base URL and model name.
  3. Test & save: Click Test connection first, then Save and use this AI. API keys are stored encrypted on the Athena server and are never shown back in full.
{% icon "lock" 18 %}

Your pictures are sent only to the AI connection you select. With local Ollama or a self-hosted vLLM/Qwen server, images never leave your network.

03 Inspections

Run a visual inspection

New inspection{% icon "arrow" 16 %}
  1. Choose what to check: On Home or New inspection, pick a ready-made visual question (such as Parking occupancy, Store foot traffic as a snapshot question, Corrosion check, or Safety gear (PPE)) or write your own. Parking and people templates interpret visible pictures; they are not live occupancy or footfall monitors.
  2. Add photos or a short video:
    • Photos tab: Drop up to 6 JPEG, PNG, or WebP images (max 20 MB each).
    • Video tab: Drop one MP4, WebM, or MOV clip. Athena automatically extracts 6 evenly spaced still frames in your browser. This is not a complete count of crossings; movements between frames can be missed. Live tracking and parking monitoring are not available in this inspection.
  3. Optionally name & run: Give the inspection a short name (or leave blank for an automatic title) and click Run inspection. Reports usually finish in 10–60 seconds and are saved even if you navigate away.

04 Regions & reports

Mark regions & read reports

All inspections{% icon "arrow" 16 %}
  1. Mark specific areas (optional): Once you add a picture in Step 2 of New inspection, the Mark areas on the picture canvas appears. Click and drag to draw boxes around specific parking bays, pipe flanges, or shelves so Athena focuses on those exact regions.
  2. Read the verdict & counts: Open any finished report from Inspections. Every report shows a clear verdict (All clear, Needs attention, or Critical), key counts, and practical next steps.
  3. Inspect findings on the image: Click any numbered finding in the list to highlight its bounding box on the photo, or toggle Show marked areas to compare your own region boxes against Athena's findings.
  4. Filter, print, re-run, or rate: Use the verdict pills and search box on Inspections to find past runs. Inside a report, rate accuracy with thumbs up/down, print it, or click Run again.

05 Custom detectors

Build a custom detector

Custom detectors{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

Each project in Custom detectors opens on an Overview checklist that highlights the single recommended next step and the role it needs:

  1. Create a project & define labels: Create an image project, then add or reorder your class labels in the Labels panel without writing code. Renaming a label updates existing boxes automatically.
  2. Import pictures & draw boxes: Upload JPEG/PNG/WebP images. Draw bounding boxes with pointer or keyboard (50–400% zoom, pan, and 100-step undo/redo), or run Auto-label (SAM + CLIP via autolabel_worker) and accept, fix, or reject proposals marked Needs review.
  3. Make a training set: Open Make a training set to check readiness, freeze an immutable numbered snapshot (deterministic 80/10/10 train/val/test split with SHA-256 manifest), and download a COCO or YOLO zip.
  4. Teach the computer: Check picture health (box counts per label and near-duplicate warnings), pick Quick or Better, and start training (train_worker). Review the plain-language results card ("found X of Y" and missed pictures).
  5. Stage, approve, or roll back: Approvers move a trained model version to trial, approve it for production check screens, or roll back to an earlier version.

06 Operations

People counting operations

People counting{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

Photo inspections answer “who is visible in these pictures?” from still frames — they cannot tell you how many people entered, because movement between frames is missed. An operation is the workflow for that: a private site and camera source, a counting line drawn on the picture, and an immutable, versioned chain of parts that will eventually replay recorded video and count crossings. It is being built in the open, and the console says exactly what works today.

  1. Where it lives: Open People counting in the sidebar (/operations). Every draft is private to its creator.
  2. Guided setup: Name the operation, add one private recording, draw a finite line, mark a few human reference crossings, then pin the parts — the source view, line geometry, counting recipe, policy, screen, and an approved detector model. The guide saves as you go and resumes after a reload.
  3. Is this server ready? The readiness list on the same page checks video decoding, private storage, the teaching and check-screen helpers, and the picture-reading model — each missing part names the person who can fix it and how.
  4. Relationship to the rest of Athena: An approved custom detector is one of the parts an operation pins; photo inspections remain the quick, no-training way to ask what is visible. Today an operation draft assembles a release candidate but cannot yet count people — recorded replay, publication and the runtime are not built yet.

07 Deployment & feedback

Check screens & improve loop

  1. Set up a check screen: From a project with an approved model, open Check screens (/projects/<id>/check-screen/setup) to create a dedicated operator screen with a shareable /check/<slug> link.
  2. Run live checks: Operators upload or capture a picture on the check screen (inference_worker) and confirm or correct the model's detections.
  3. Pull corrections back in (Improve loop): A Labeler opens Corrections from check screens inside the project to import operator-corrected pictures once as Needs review boxes—keeping lineage while never overwriting already-reviewed data.

10 FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers

Can Athena OS run completely offline on my own server?

Yes. Connect a local Ollama or vLLM vision server running Qwen (or another open-weight vision-language model) in AI model settings, and all images and reports stay inside your network.

What is the difference between an instant inspection and a custom detector?

Instant inspections use a vision-language model to answer plain-language questions about up to 6 photos or a short video with zero training data. Custom detectors let you label your own images, freeze a dataset snapshot, and train a dedicated model for high-speed, repeatable check screens.

What image and video formats does Athena OS support?

Instant inspections accept up to 6 JPEG, PNG, or WebP photos (up to 20 MB each) or one MP4, WebM, or MOV video clip (from which 6 evenly spaced frames are extracted automatically).

How are API keys stored when I connect my own AI provider?

API keys are encrypted on the Athena server before being stored in the database and are never displayed back in full after saving.

11 Product direction · 17 November 2026 review

Multimodal capability roadmap

Proposed priorities

Athena OS is expanding from computer vision into a general-purpose, open-source, self-hostable AI training operating system. Instead of building separate silos per data format, every modality shares one workspace, one guided training engine, and one governance lifecycle. Proprietary services such as Athena Forge remain optional through compatible, versioned interfaces where licensing permits.

Athena ForgeAdvanced training engines, experiments, specialized algorithms and existing ML integrations (reused via compatible interfaces where licensing permits).
Athena LabResearch, expert review, benchmarking, governance and release qualification.
Athena NodesOptional distributed CPU/GPU computing and job execution after integration tests.
Domain PacksReusable industry-specific tasks, datasets, evaluation rules and deployment templates.

12 Engineering execution & governance

Delivery waves, task hierarchy & quality gates

Waves 0 to 4

The roadmap organizes delivery into five waves (indicative sequence, not committed release dates). The recommended next milestone is Athena OS Multimodal MVP: four real, independently validated training workflows (Vision, Acoustics, Handwriting OCR, and Tabular prediction) sharing one lifecycle.

Five delivery waves

WaveHorizonScope & key requirements
Wave 0 — Shared foundation0–6 weeksOS-CORE-001 to 006: task/recipe registry, modality plugins, shared observation/dataset/annotation contracts, durable job recovery, model registry, universal guided workflow.
Wave 1 — Multi-task MVP2–4 monthsOS-VIS-001 (Vision detection), OS-AUD-001 (Acoustic classifier), OS-OCR-001 (Handwriting TrOCR), OS-TAB-001 (Excel/CSV tabular prediction).
Wave 2 — Language & signals4–7 monthsOS-ASR-001/002 (Speech dataset editor & ASR fine-tuning), OS-NLP-001 (Text/NER), OS-DOC-001 (Document fields/tables), OS-SIG-001 (Time-series), OS-AUD-002 (Normal-only acoustic anomaly).
Wave 3 — Motion & operations7–10 monthsPose/keypoint annotation, gesture/action models, video event labeling, industry Domain Packs, operational dashboards, and consent-gated biometric verification.
Wave 4 — Multimodal & edge10–15+ monthsSynchronized cross-modal fusion (audio + vibration/temp, video + pose, thermal + RGB), modality ablations, qualified edge deployments and optional Athena Nodes.

Platform-wide quality gates

Every training run preserves dataset/license provenance, label-review status, immutable splits, training configuration, hardware details, model version, evaluation evidence and approval history. A model is never presented as production-ready merely because training finished.

Task typePrimary quality checks
Object detectionRecall, precision, mAP, false positives
Acoustic eventsEvent F1, missed events, false alarms per hour
Speech recognitionWord error rate (WER), character error rate (CER), speaker/dialect generalization
OCRCharacter/word errors, field accuracy where applicable
Text classificationClass F1, confusion analysis, false classifications
Pose estimationKeypoint accuracy, OKS/PCK and visibility failures
Time-series anomaliesEvent recall, false-alert rate, detection delay
Face verificationFalse acceptance/rejection rates and subgroup testing

Immediate execution priorities

  2. Finish OS T05 and the shared training-job lifecycle: Versioned release bundles, durable execution, artifact and model provenance.
  3. Create universal task/recipe and annotation plugin interfaces: Make sound, OCR and spreadsheet tasks first-class without duplicating the platform.
  4. Unify the beginner-friendly Teach workflow: One guided experience with appropriate controls for each task.
  5. Validate four real-data vertical slices: Vision, acoustic classification, handwriting OCR and spreadsheets.
  6. Complete evaluation, approval, deployment and recovery gates: End-to-end quality and operational truth before expanding further.
