00 Overview

How Athena OS works

Start here

Athena OS is a self-hosted visual inspection workspace with two ways to check pictures and video—pick the one that fits your job:

{% icon "sparkle" 18 %}1. Instant visual inspections

Ask a question in plain words, add up to 6 photos or a short video, and get a plain-language report with verdicts, counts, marked regions, and next steps. No training data needed.

Start an inspection{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

{% icon "detector" 18 %}2. Custom detectors & check screens

Label your own pictures, freeze a training set, teach a dedicated detector on your hardware, and publish a fast one-click operator check screen with a human-in-the-loop improve cycle.

Open custom detectors{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

01 Getting started

First-run setup & sign-in

Admins & operators
  1. Complete first-run onboarding: On a brand-new install with no users yet, visiting the app opens the guided setup screen (/setup/). Enter the first administrator email, username, and password.
  2. Pick your starting AI option: During setup, choose Local Qwen (Ollama) (defaults to http://127.0.0.1:11434/v1), enter a Custom AI endpoint, or select Skip for now and configure it later in settings.
  3. Sign in anytime: Returning users sign in at /login/. Repeated failed sign-in attempts temporarily lock out the IP/username to protect your server.
  4. Switch workspace theme: Use Control-room mode at the bottom of the left sidebar to toggle between the bright day theme and the dark control-room theme.

02 AI configuration

Connect your AI model

Open AI model{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

Before running an instant inspection, Athena needs a vision-language model (a model that can read images). The status dot next to AI model in the sidebar turns green when it is answering.

  1. Open AI model settings: Click AI model in the sidebar (/settings/ai).
  2. Choose Built-in Qwen or Your own AI:
    • Built-in Qwen: Uses the organisation-wide server configured by your Athena administrator. Click Test the built-in model to verify it responds.
    • Your own AI: Connect any OpenAI-compatible /v1/chat/completions vision service (OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter, Ollama, vLLM, or LM Studio). Pick a preset or enter the base URL and model name.
  3. Test & save: Click Test connection first, then Save and use this AI. API keys are stored encrypted on the Athena server and are never shown back in full.
{% icon "lock" 18 %}

Your pictures are sent only to the AI connection you select. With local Ollama or a self-hosted vLLM/Qwen server, images never leave your network.

03 Inspections

Run a visual inspection

New inspection{% icon "arrow" 16 %}
  1. Choose what to check: On Home or New inspection, pick a ready-made visual question (such as Parking occupancy, Store foot traffic as a snapshot question, Corrosion check, or Safety gear (PPE)) or write your own. Parking and people templates interpret visible pictures; they are not live occupancy or footfall monitors.
  2. Add photos or a short video:
    • Photos tab: Drop up to 6 JPEG, PNG, or WebP images (max 20 MB each).
    • Video tab: Drop one MP4, WebM, or MOV clip. Athena automatically extracts 6 evenly spaced still frames in your browser. This is not a complete count of crossings; movements between frames can be missed. Live tracking and parking monitoring are not available in this inspection.
  3. Optionally name & run: Give the inspection a short name (or leave blank for an automatic title) and click Run inspection. Reports usually finish in 10–60 seconds and are saved even if you navigate away.

04 Regions & reports

Mark regions & read reports

All inspections{% icon "arrow" 16 %}
  1. Mark specific areas (optional): Once you add a picture in Step 2 of New inspection, the Mark areas on the picture canvas appears. Click and drag to draw boxes around specific parking bays, pipe flanges, or shelves so Athena focuses on those exact regions.
  2. Read the verdict & counts: Open any finished report from Inspections. Every report shows a clear verdict (All clear, Needs attention, or Critical), key counts, and practical next steps.
  3. Inspect findings on the image: Click any numbered finding in the list to highlight its bounding box on the photo, or toggle Show marked areas to compare your own region boxes against Athena's findings.
  4. Filter, print, re-run, or rate: Use the verdict pills and search box on Inspections to find past runs. Inside a report, rate accuracy with thumbs up/down, print it, or click Run again.

05 Custom detectors

Build a custom detector

Custom detectors{% icon "arrow" 16 %}

Each project in Custom detectors opens on an Overview checklist that highlights the single recommended next step and the role it needs:

  1. Create a project & define labels: Create an image project, then add or reorder your class labels in the Labels panel without writing code. Renaming a label updates existing boxes automatically.
  2. Import pictures & draw boxes: Upload JPEG/PNG/WebP images. Draw bounding boxes with pointer or keyboard (50–400% zoom, pan, and 100-step undo/redo), or run Auto-label (SAM + CLIP via autolabel_worker) and accept, fix, or reject proposals marked Needs review.
  3. Make a training set: Open Make a training set to check readiness, freeze an immutable numbered snapshot (deterministic 80/10/10 train/val/test split with SHA-256 manifest), and download a COCO or YOLO zip.
  4. Teach the computer: Check picture health (box counts per label and near-duplicate warnings), pick Quick or Better, and start training (train_worker). Review the plain-language results card ("found X of Y" and missed pictures).
  5. Stage, approve, or roll back: Approvers move a trained model version to trial, approve it for production check screens, or roll back to an earlier version.

06 Deployment & feedback

Check screens & improve loop

  1. Set up a check screen: From a project with an approved model, open Check screens (/projects/<id>/check-screen/setup) to create a dedicated operator screen with a shareable /check/<slug> link.
  2. Run live checks: Operators upload or capture a picture on the check screen (inference_worker) and confirm or correct the model's detections.
  3. Pull corrections back in (Improve loop): A Labeler opens Corrections from check screens inside the project to import operator-corrected pictures once as Needs review boxes—keeping lineage while never overwriting already-reviewed data.

07 Governance

Team access & roles

{% if athena_access.can_administer %}Open Access{% icon "arrow" 16 %}{% endif %}

Every write action in Athena OS is enforced on the server by role. Administrators (superusers) assign console-wide or per-project roles on the Access page (/access):

RoleWhat they can do
ViewerRead inspections, projects, datasets, and docs; cannot create or modify records.
LabelerRun inspections, connect personal AI settings, import images, draw/review boxes, and import check-screen corrections.
TrainerEverything a Labeler can do, plus freeze dataset snapshots, start training runs, and configure check screens.
ApproverEverything a Trainer can do, plus stage, approve, and roll back model versions.
AdministratorSuperuser access to assign roles, inspect the role-change audit log, and toggle ready-made model downloads.

09 FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers

Can Athena OS run completely offline on my own server?

Yes. Connect a local Ollama or vLLM vision server running Qwen (or another open-weight vision-language model) in AI model settings, and all images and reports stay inside your network.

What is the difference between an instant inspection and a custom detector?

Instant inspections use a vision-language model to answer plain-language questions about up to 6 photos or a short video with zero training data. Custom detectors let you label your own images, freeze a dataset snapshot, and train a dedicated model for high-speed, repeatable check screens.

What image and video formats does Athena OS support?

Instant inspections accept up to 6 JPEG, PNG, or WebP photos (up to 20 MB each) or one MP4, WebM, or MOV video clip (from which 6 evenly spaced frames are extracted automatically).

How are API keys stored when I connect my own AI provider?

API keys are encrypted on the Athena server before being stored in the database and are never displayed back in full after saving.
